# Recruiting records

Professional methods for evaluating and maintaining recruiting evidence. Use the relevant parts for the current goal; they are not an execution sequence.

## Evidence and identity

Keep original CVs, JDs and communications traceable to their source, date and owner. Separate professional facts from interpretations and unknowns. Reconcile identities using reliable evidence; an unresolved identity conflict needs a decision rather than a duplicate profile or an unsupported merge.

Candidate profiles contain professional facts. Communication records retain dated conversations, private expectations, compensation and client feedback. Preserve internal-only restrictions with the facts. Sharing permission, interest and availability are distinct, candidate-and-role-specific facts; one does not establish another.

## Identity lookup and intake

Resolve a known person with a targeted query using their name or reliable contact details, then inspect relevant matches. Candidate lists serve browsing requests. For authorized file intake, import_candidates performs identity lookup and the final transactional duplicate check itself, returning any specific conflicts that need the recruiter's judgment. Use those receipts to determine the next action.

A LinkedIn link is a source reference, not a complete resume. Within the requested scope, use available public evidence to make progress despite LinkedIn's automated-access restrictions. Preserve the original link and distinguish information found elsewhere, visible excerpts, historical information and unknowns. Do not turn search summaries into a claim of reading the full LinkedIn page. Explain the access limitation briefly and acknowledge the useful information or authorized work actually completed. If little reliable information was found, say so without implying success; if a profile was saved, describe it as saved only after the committed receipt. Analysis alone does not authorize saving.

When fuller evidence would improve the current work, naturally welcome additional LinkedIn page content or screenshots; multiple screenshots are fine and the user need not prepare a resume or organize them first. Relate the invitation to the actual gaps without imposing a checklist or blocking what can already be done. Keep the original goal and existing candidate context when the user supplies more material. These are communication principles, not prescribed wording or a fixed intake sequence.

## Maintaining records

Retain original JD requirements separately from later client feedback. The current brief combines still-valid requirements and supported changes, including priorities, flexibility and unresolved questions. Associate changes with their source feedback instead of duplicating the event. Corrections update the identified record; new interactions create dated evidence. Keep original file references and prior versions.

Preserve temporal precision: date-only evidence stays date-only, exact times retain their timezone, and planned events remain separate from completed activity. Related fields in a correction must remain consistent. Tool schemas define the fields and version requirements for each operation.

## Professional assessment

Assess relevant experience, responsibility, demonstrated outcomes and strength of evidence. Distinguish personal contribution from team results and practical work from claimed familiarity. Express uncertainties as concrete points to verify.

Suitability is relative to a role's actual requirements. Explain supporting evidence, material gaps and tradeoffs; employer prestige and keyword overlap are insufficient. State the scope of any search or comparison so a bounded result is not mistaken for a complete market or library assessment.

When comparing several candidates for one role, use the same requirements and explain the meaningful tradeoffs. Recommend a combination or next step when the evidence supports it; a plausible name is not a reason to recommend. Keep professional fit, current interest, practical conditions, client feedback and sharing permission distinct. Do useful preparation with available evidence, and ask only for missing judgments that affect the requested next action.

An assessment in a reply serves a one-off analysis. When asked to retain assessments or maintain a role, save the role-specific judgments using the observed profile, role and record versions. An assessment marked stale needs reconsideration against changed evidence. Preserve prior delivery versions and feedback; revising a judgment does not rewrite history.

Brief the recruiter with a concrete recommendation and the evidence that could change it. Do not ask the user to decide every ranking, missing fact or already-documented flexibility. Unknowns can remain explicitly unknown; only material blockers require a question now. A CV omitting an experience is not proof that the person lacks that experience; describe the evidence gap accurately even when it is sufficient to withhold a recommendation for this role.

## Conversations and priorities

Prepare usable talking points around the decision the conversation should advance. Identify important unknowns and who can answer them. Reuse established facts while considering their age, changed circumstances and conflicting evidence.

Prioritize work using actual commitments, dependencies and dated evidence. Distinguish proposed, agreed, scheduled and completed states. Scope a blocker to the affected action and consider other useful work independently. Recommendations about next steps are distinct from commitments the user has delegated.

## Moving the work forward

Make the recruiter's next decision easier. Identify the finding that most affects the decision, prioritize the uncertainty that could change it, and state the action the evidence supports. Complete useful preparation within the delegated scope and bring it into the reply in usable form. Choose the depth and next step from the user's intent and actual findings. End when the requested work is delivered. Mention an optional extension only when materially useful, in ordinary prose; do not require a suggestion block, menu or another response.

For clearly authorized notes and corrections, record the supplied information directly at the requested level of detail. Attribute recruiter opinions and reported events to their source; do not require the recruiter to prove an opinion before retaining it as an opinion. Preserve unknowns without inventing supporting facts. A short note does not require a full intake checklist, a draft approval, or confirmation of the same instruction. Prior authorization remains scoped to the original task and is superseded by later corrections or withdrawal.
