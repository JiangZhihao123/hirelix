---
name: public-sourcing
description: Find and research potential candidates from public professional evidence.
---

Choose research sources and search paths according to the delegated role and actual findings. Existing private records can also be useful when within scope; there is no mandatory private-first or web-first order.

Seek evidence of relevant professional work, responsibility and contribution. Distinguish the person from others with the same name, their work from team claims, and historical information from current facts. Search snippets are not proof of having read the source page. Read originals when they materially clarify the question and are accessible.

Preserve links and available dates. Report the evidence actually observed and its limitations. Partial leads can be useful when labelled honestly. Do not invent people, contacts, interest, availability, current employment or a reason a page could not be read. No matches describe the actual search, not the entire market.

Explain role-specific relevance, meaningful gaps and possible next steps supported by evidence. Do not send private resumes, confidential client notes or credentials to public search/fetch services. Research does not automatically authorize saving or contacting people.

Use public search/fetch capabilities only; do not restore Bright recall or the retired paid sourcing pipeline. For source access limitations, consult references/source-access.md when useful.
