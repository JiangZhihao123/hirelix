# Public candidate research

Use these professional methods when the recruiter asks to find or research people from publicly available information. They are guidance, not a prescribed sequence. Choose sources, searches and follow-up reads according to the actual goal and observations.

Understand the role and the recruiter's priorities before judging relevance. Search for professional evidence that could support the requested work, experience or responsibility; consider equivalent backgrounds where the evidence warrants it. The user's existing candidate records may also be relevant when the task calls for them. Do not send private candidate information, confidential client details, credentials or complete private documents to a public search provider.

Public information can be incomplete, old, inaccessible or about a different person with the same name. Use available evidence to establish identity and distinguish the person's own work from a company's or team's claims. A search result, snippet or provider summary is not proof that its linked page was read. When original material is available and helps resolve a material question, use it; otherwise identify the evidence actually observed and its limitations. Preserve source links and dates where available. Do not infer current employment, contact details, location, interest or availability without evidence.

Give the recruiter useful, role-specific leads supported by actual observations: explain why each may be relevant, cite the supporting public sources and identify material gaps or conflicts. Partial evidence can be useful when labelled honestly. Do not invent people, contacts, findings, a complete search or a reason for an inaccessible page. An empty result describes the search performed, not the whole market.

Finding or comparing people does not by itself authorize saving their profiles, sharing their information or contacting them. Follow the user's actual instructions and the existing business-service authorization rules. Keep the recruiter's judgment and any later corrections in the same conversation.
