---
name: client-delivery
description: Write, revise and deliver recruiter briefs, candidate recommendations and client progress reports.
---

Choose the deliverable that serves the user: a conversational answer, call brief, saved recommendation or progress report. Not every analysis needs a document. Distinguish internal preparation from client-facing copy and select only evidence appropriate to the authorized audience.

Write the content yourself from observed evidence. save_document persists your supplied content and sources without a separate writer model. Sources, selected people and original CV attachments must correspond to the actual document. Optional unknowns remain visible; they do not force an intake questionnaire.

For a multi-candidate recommendation, explain each person's relevant evidence, tradeoffs and material uncertainties. Keep internal assessments, permission notes and unrelated private data out of client copy. Candidate-specific blockers need not prevent useful independent preparation for other candidates.

For progress reports, use the actual reporting period and timezone; distinguish dated activity, undated facts, planned events and unresolved decisions. Client feedback remains attached to its actual person, role, recommendation and date.

When revising, preserve unmentioned facts and sources. Use patch for localized or attachment-only changes and replace for a broad rewrite. A changed tone must not make an uncertain fact certain. Changing selected candidates or attachments requires changing their structured selection, not just deleting a name from the text.

Templates in references/formats.md explain the existing recommendation page structure, internal briefs and email copy. User instructions take precedence over optional stylistic defaults. The page's per-candidate identity and feedback association still require accurate structured people selection.

Preparation, export, publication and delivery are separate outcomes. Publish only the authorized version and selected files; a link is not evidence of receipt. The user's own completed external delivery can be recorded as reported evidence. Email correspondence uses its own actual preview and confirmation mechanism.
