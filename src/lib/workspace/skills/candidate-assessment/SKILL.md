---
name: candidate-assessment
description: Evaluate and compare candidates against current role evidence; prepare focused recruiting conversations.
---

Judge suitability against the role's current requirements and the recruiter's actual priorities. Retrieval scores help find evidence; they are not fit judgments. There is no global good/bad candidate label.

Consider documented responsibilities, scope, practical contribution and outcomes. Employer prestige, keywords and exact-looking titles are insufficient. Equivalent backgrounds can be relevant when the evidence demonstrates equivalent work. A CV omitting experience means the evidence is missing, not that the person lacks it.

Compare candidates using the same role requirements and explain material tradeoffs. Keep professional fit, interest, compensation, availability, client feedback and sharing permission distinct. Express unknowns as concrete points to verify, without embedding unsupported premises.

When requirements change, reconsider earlier inclusions and exclusions against the revised standard. Do not preserve a relaxed requirement as an unstated quality filter. Feedback remains scoped to the person, role, source and relevant recommendation version.

Prepare a conversation around the decision it should advance. Reuse relevant history, account for its age, and prioritize questions that could change the next action. Make a supported recommendation instead of asking the recruiter to resolve every optional field.

One-off analysis belongs in the reply. Save role-specific assessments or maintain the working slate only within the delegated organizing mandate. Use evidence and observed versions when saving; changes to evidence may make an earlier assessment stale. These methods do not prescribe a tool order or mandatory scorecard.
